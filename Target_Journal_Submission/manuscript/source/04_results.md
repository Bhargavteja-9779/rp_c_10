## Cohorts
The development pool comprised {{D:dev_total|patients}} patients ({{D:dev_total|hours}} h of analysed RR data; {{D:dev_total|patients_af}} with AF), and the external cohorts {{D:ext_total|patients}} patients ({{D:ext_total|hours}} h; {{D:ext_total|patients_af}} with AF and {{D:ext_total|patients_afl}} with atrial flutter): SHDB-AF {{D:shdb-af|patients}} patients ({{D:shdb-af|patients_af}} with AF during the recording), CPSC2021 {{D:cpsc2021|patients}} patients and Icentia11k {{D:icentia11k|patients}} patients ({{D:icentia11k|patients_af}} with AF) (Table 1; Supplementary Figure S1). ## Window-level detection
All three detectors discriminated AF windows well in every cohort (Supplementary Table S2, Supplementary Figures S2–S3). For LightGBM, the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve was {{wm:LightGBM|dev_test|auroc}} in the development test set and {{wm:LightGBM|ext_shdb-af|auroc}}, {{wm:LightGBM|ext_cpsc2021|auroc}} and {{wm:LightGBM|ext_icentia11k|auroc}} in SHDB-AF, CPSC2021 and Icentia11k; sensitivity and specificity at the default threshold were {{wmm:LightGBM|ext_shdb-af|sensitivity}} and {{wmm:LightGBM|ext_shdb-af|specificity}} in SHDB-AF and {{wmm:LightGBM|ext_icentia11k|sensitivity}} and {{wmm:LightGBM|ext_icentia11k|specificity}} in Icentia11k.

## Burden estimation
By the pre-specified development-data rule (Amendment A1), the PA-HMMQ variant using the detector probability as emission likelihood had a lower development error ({{S:amendment_A1|dev_mae_raw_emission}} vs {{S:amendment_A1|dev_mae_pre_registered}} pp) and was designated the final method; the pre-registered calibrated-emission variant is reported alongside.

With the primary LightGBM detector, the final PA-HMMQ had the lowest mean absolute burden error of all estimators in the development test set and in each external cohort (Table 2, Figure 2): {{mae:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]|ext_shdb-af}} pp in SHDB-AF, {{mae:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]|ext_cpsc2021}} pp in CPSC2021 and {{mae:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]|ext_icentia11k}} pp in Icentia11k, compared with {{mae:LightGBM|CC|ext_shdb-af}}, {{mae:LightGBM|CC|ext_cpsc2021}} and {{mae:LightGBM|CC|ext_icentia11k}} pp for classify-and-count. In the pooled external cohorts the error was {{mae:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]|ext_pooled}} pp versus {{mae:LightGBM|CC|ext_pooled}} pp for classify-and-count, {{mae:LightGBM|CC-MF|ext_pooled}} pp for median-filtered counting, {{mae:LightGBM|HMM-src|ext_pooled}} pp for a hidden Markov model with fixed dynamics and {{mae:LightGBM|PCC-iso|ext_pooled}} pp for isotonic-recalibrated probability averaging. In the primary paired analysis, the final PA-HMMQ reduced the per-patient error relative to every comparator (Table 4; Supplementary Figure S6); the mean paired reduction was {{test:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]|ext_pooled|CC|mean_diff}} pp (95% CI {{test:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]|ext_pooled|CC|mean_ci}}) versus classify-and-count and {{test:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]|ext_pooled|HMM-src|mean_diff}} pp (95% CI {{test:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]|ext_pooled|HMM-src|mean_ci}}) versus the fixed-dynamics HMM (Holm-adjusted P ≤ {{test:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]|ext_pooled|CC|p_holm}} for all comparators). Because most external patients were AF-free and many estimators returned exactly zero burden for them, the median paired difference was close to zero; the improvement was concentrated in patients with AF ({{bm:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]|ext_pooled|mae_af_present_pp}} vs {{bm:LightGBM|CC|ext_pooled|mae_af_present_pp}} pp) and in AF-free patients with false-positive windows (mean false burden {{bm:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]|ext_pooled|af_free_mean_false_burden_pp}} vs {{bm:LightGBM|CC|ext_pooled|af_free_mean_false_burden_pp}} pp; Table 3).

The ranking was reproduced with the Classical-LR and RRNet detectors (Supplementary Tables S3–S4): pooled external error {{mae:Classical-LR|PA-HMMQ[rawE]|ext_pooled}} vs {{mae:Classical-LR|CC|ext_pooled}} pp (Classical-LR) and {{mae:RRNet|PA-HMMQ[rawE]|ext_pooled}} vs {{mae:RRNet|CC|ext_pooled}} pp (RRNet). Agreement statistics are given in Table 3 and Figure 3: bias was {{bm:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]|ext_pooled|bias_pp}} pp (classify-and-count {{bm:LightGBM|CC|ext_pooled|bias_pp}} pp) and Lin's concordance correlation {{bm:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]|ext_pooled|ccc|3}} ({{bm:LightGBM|CC|ext_pooled|ccc|3}}). Patient classification by burden thresholds is reported in Supplementary Table S7.

## Ablation
Supplementary Table S5 and Supplementary Figure S5 decompose the gain. Per-patient prior adaptation without a temporal model (EMQ) did not improve on classify-and-count externally ({{bm:LightGBM|EMQ|ext_pooled|mae_pp}} pp), whereas a temporal model with fixed source dynamics did ({{bm:LightGBM|HMM-src|ext_pooled|mae_pp}} pp with calibrated emissions; {{bm:LightGBM|HMM-src[rawE]|ext_pooled|mae_pp}} pp with probability emissions). Adding per-patient adaptation of the dynamics changed the error to {{bm:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]|ext_pooled|mae_pp}} pp with probability emissions. The error varied little across prior strengths κ from 1 to 100 (Supplementary Table S5). With calibration-set emission histograms, the PA-HMMQ was not better than the fixed-dynamics HMM ({{bm:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ|ext_pooled|mae_pp}} vs {{bm:LightGBM|HMM-src|ext_pooled|mae_pp}} pp).

## Uncertainty intervals
Normalised conformal intervals around the final PA-HMMQ covered the reference burden in {{iv:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]+conformal(norm)|dev_test|coverage}} of development test patients and {{iv:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]+conformal(norm)|ext_pooled|coverage}} (95% CI {{iv:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]+conformal(norm)|ext_pooled|cp95}}) of external patients ({{iv:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]+conformal(norm)|ext_shdb-af|coverage}} SHDB-AF, {{iv:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]+conformal(norm)|ext_cpsc2021|coverage}} CPSC2021, {{iv:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]+conformal(norm)|ext_icentia11k|coverage}} Icentia11k), with a median width of {{iv:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]+conformal(norm)|ext_pooled|median_width_pp|2}} pp (Table 5, Figure 4). Coverage was lower in patients with AF ({{iv:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]+conformal(norm)|ext_pooled|coverage_af}}) than in AF-free patients ({{iv:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]+conformal(norm)|ext_pooled|coverage_af_free}}). Constant-width conformal intervals around classify-and-count were wider (median {{iv:LightGBM|CC+conformal(const)|ext_pooled|median_width_pp|2}} pp) with coverage {{iv:LightGBM|CC+conformal(const)|ext_pooled|coverage}}, and the uncalibrated posterior credible intervals under-covered ({{iv:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ posterior 90% CrI|ext_pooled|coverage}}).

## Robustness and sensitivity analyses
In SHDB-AF, all estimators deteriorated with false R peaks and simulated premature beats, while missed beats and timing jitter had smaller effects (Figure 5; Supplementary Table S9). Training on 25% or 50% of the training patients and using 30- or 120-beat windows are reported in Supplementary Table S11.

## Error analysis
Errors were concentrated in patients with intermediate burden and in a small number of patients with atrial flutter (Figure 6; Supplementary Tables S6, S13). Of the {{D:ext_total|patients}} external patients, {{afl:CC|n_with_afl}} had flutter; counting flutter as arrhythmia reduced the error of the final PA-HMMQ to {{afl:PA-HMMQ[rawE]|mae_afafl}} pp, and excluding these patients (post hoc) to {{afl:PA-HMMQ[rawE]|mae_no_afl}} pp (classify-and-count {{afl:CC|mae_afafl}} and {{afl:CC|mae_no_afl}} pp; Supplementary Table S8). Absolute error correlated with the proportion of flutter (Spearman ρ = {{S:error_correlations|afl|rho}}) and with the reference burden (ρ = {{S:error_correlations|true_burden|rho}}), and only weakly with ectopic beats (supraventricular ρ = {{S:error_correlations|ectopy_sv|rho}}; ventricular ρ = {{S:error_correlations|ectopy_v|rho}}; Supplementary Figure S7). Error did not differ materially by sex or age in SHDB-AF (Supplementary Table S12).
