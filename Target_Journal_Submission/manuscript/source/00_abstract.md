# Abstract

**Background and Aims** Atrial fibrillation (AF) burden is increasingly used to guide care, yet automated tools estimate it by counting windows classified as AF—a biased estimator of a proportion—and report no uncertainty. We aimed to develop and externally validate a detector-agnostic burden estimator that corrects this bias and provides per-patient uncertainty intervals.

**Methods** Burden was framed as a per-patient quantification problem. A patient-adaptive hidden Markov quantifier (PA-HMMQ) re-estimates each patient's rhythm dynamics from the output of any beat-to-beat (RR) interval AF detector; split-conformal calibration yields 90% burden intervals. Three detectors were developed on {{D:dev_total|patients}} patients ({{D:dev_total|hours}} h; six US databases) and validated, without retraining or recalibration, in {{D:ext_total|patients}} patients ({{D:ext_total|hours}} h) from Japan, China and Canada. The pre-specified primary endpoint was the per-patient absolute burden error in pooled external cohorts.

**Results** With the primary detector, the mean absolute burden error in external patients was {{mae:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]|ext_pooled}} percentage points (pp) for PA-HMMQ versus {{mae:LightGBM|CC|ext_pooled}} pp for classify-and-count (mean paired difference {{test:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]|ext_pooled|CC|mean_diff}} pp, 95% CI {{test:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]|ext_pooled|CC|mean_ci}}; Holm-adjusted P = {{test:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]|ext_pooled|CC|p_holm}}), and it was lower than all eight comparators, including established quantification and smoothing methods. Conformal intervals covered the reference burden in {{iv:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]+conformal(norm)|ext_pooled|coverage}} of external patients (target 0.90) with a median width of {{iv:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]+conformal(norm)|ext_pooled|median_width_pp|2}} pp. The largest residual errors arose in patients with atrial flutter, which RR-based detectors classified as AF, and from false burden in AF-free patients.

**Conclusion** Treating AF burden as a quantity to be estimated, rather than a count of classified windows, modestly but consistently reduced burden error across three continents and enabled calibrated uncertainty intervals. Flutter and false-positive burden in AF-free patients remain the main limitations of RR-based burden estimation.
