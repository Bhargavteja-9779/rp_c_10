# Abstract

**Background and Aims** Automated tools estimate atrial fibrillation (AF) burden by counting windows classified as AF—a biased estimator of a proportion—without uncertainty. We developed and externally validated a detector-agnostic burden estimator with per-patient uncertainty intervals.

**Methods** A patient-adaptive hidden Markov burden layer converts the output of any beat-to-beat interval AF detector into burden, modelling the persistence of rhythm and optionally re-estimating each patient's rhythm dynamics; split-conformal calibration gives 90% intervals. Three detectors were developed in {{D:dev_total|patients}} patients (USA) and evaluated without recalibration in {{D:ext_total|patients}} patients from Japan, China and Canada. The pre-registered primary endpoint was the per-patient absolute burden error in pooled external cohorts.

**Results** The pre-registered specification did not outperform classify-and-count ({{mae:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ|ext_pooled}} vs {{mae:LightGBM|CC|ext_pooled}} percentage points [pp]). With emissions selected on development data by a rule specified before the full analysis, the error was {{mae:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]|ext_pooled}} pp (paired difference {{test:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]|ext_pooled|CC|mean_diff}} pp, 95% CI {{test:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]|ext_pooled|CC|mean_ci}}), lower on average than all eight comparators and with all three detectors, although rank tests were not significant against three comparators. Temporal modelling, not per-patient re-estimation of dynamics, explained the gain, and a tuned median filter recovered most of it. Specificity for AF presence (≥30 s) rose from {{bm:LightGBM|CC|ext_pooled|present_30s_spec|2}} to {{bm:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]|ext_pooled|present_30s_spec|2}} while sensitivity fell from {{bm:LightGBM|CC|ext_pooled|present_30s_sens|2}} to {{bm:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]|ext_pooled|present_30s_sens|2}}. Conformal intervals covered {{iv:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]+conformal(norm)|ext_pooled|coverage|2}} of external patients (target 0.90) but {{iv:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]+conformal(norm)|ext_shdb-af|coverage|2}} in the Japanese cohort. Atrial flutter caused the largest errors.

**Conclusion** Modelling rhythm persistence when aggregating detector outputs modestly reduced burden error and spurious AF across three continents, and conformal intervals achieved near-nominal coverage in two of three external cohorts. Flutter remains the main limitation of RR-based burden estimation.
