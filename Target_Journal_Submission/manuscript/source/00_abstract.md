# Abstract

**Background and Aims** Atrial fibrillation (AF) burden is increasingly used to guide care, yet automated tools estimate it by counting time windows classified as AF—a biased estimator of a proportion—and report no uncertainty. We aimed to develop and externally validate a detector-agnostic burden estimator with per-patient uncertainty intervals.

**Methods** Burden was treated as a per-patient quantity to be estimated. A hidden Markov burden layer (PA-HMMQ) uses the output of any beat-to-beat interval AF detector, models the temporal persistence of rhythm, optionally re-estimates each patient's rhythm dynamics, and provides split-conformal 90% intervals. Three detectors were developed in {{D:dev_total|patients}} patients (six US databases) and evaluated, without retraining or recalibration, in {{D:ext_total|patients}} patients ({{D:ext_total|hours}} h) from Japan, China and Canada. The analysis plan was pre-registered; the pre-specified primary endpoint was the per-patient absolute burden error in pooled external cohorts.

**Results** With the pre-registered emission model, PA-HMMQ did not outperform classify-and-count ({{mae:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ|ext_pooled}} vs {{mae:LightGBM|CC|ext_pooled}} percentage points [pp]). With the emission model selected on development data before the full analysis, the mean error was {{mae:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]|ext_pooled}} pp (mean paired difference vs classify-and-count {{test:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]|ext_pooled|CC|mean_diff}} pp, 95% CI {{test:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]|ext_pooled|CC|mean_ci}}), lower than all eight comparators on average and for all three detectors, although rank-based tests were not significant against three of them. The gain came from temporal modelling; per-patient re-estimation of dynamics added nothing. Specificity for AF presence (≥30 s) rose from {{bm:LightGBM|CC|ext_pooled|present_30s_spec|2}} to {{bm:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]|ext_pooled|present_30s_spec|2}} at the cost of sensitivity ({{bm:LightGBM|CC|ext_pooled|present_30s_sens|2}} to {{bm:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]|ext_pooled|present_30s_sens|2}}). Conformal intervals covered {{iv:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]+conformal(norm)|ext_pooled|coverage|2}} of external patients (target 0.90), but only {{iv:LightGBM|PA-HMMQ[rawE]+conformal(norm)|ext_shdb-af|coverage|2}} in the Japanese cohort. Atrial flutter caused the largest errors.

**Conclusion** Modelling rhythm persistence when aggregating detector outputs modestly reduced AF-burden error and false AF diagnoses across three continents and enabled calibrated per-patient intervals; atrial flutter remains the main limitation of RR-based burden estimation.
